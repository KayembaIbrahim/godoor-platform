## A. WAITING / STOPPAGE TIME CHARGES

### A1. Uber — official global waiting-time policy

- **Free wait at pickup = 2 minutes (UberX); 5 minutes (Uber Black / Black SUV).** Exact wording: *"In some locations,
  a rider will be charged a per-minute wait time fee, which will begin 2 minutes after the driver-partner arrives at the
  rider's location. We notify the rider that the charge has started, and it will continue until the driver begins the
  trip. EXTENDED WAIT TIME - Uber Black: Per-minute wait time fees kick in after 5 minutes (except on Airports where it
  still begins after 2 minutes)."* — **CONFIRMED**, https://help.uber.com/en/riders/article/wait-time-fees, retrieved 2026-10-07.

- **Uber publishes NO global rate formula.** Exact wording: *"This fee will begin accumulating 2 minutes (5 minutes for
  Black or Black SUV trips) after your driver arrives. The fee is based on the per-minute wait time rate that is
  applicable in that city. To see the per-minute wait time rate, ensure you are logged in and run a price estimate.
  Select a vehicle type and then click the info button to see a complete fare breakdown with the per minute wait time
  fee charge below."* — **CONFIRMED**, https://help.uber.com/en/riders/article/your-trip-details, retrieved 2026-10-07.

- **Waiver rule:** *"If the trip is cancelled and the rider is charged a cancellation or no-show fee, they will not be
  charged for wait time. Additionally, this fee may not apply to airports or certain other venues."* — **CONFIRMED**,
  same URL as above.

- **Which Uber cities publish a per-minute rate in local currency? → NOT FOUND.** Uber's own documentation states the
  rate is surfaced only in-app (price estimate → vehicle info → fare breakdown). There is no public per-city rate table
  to cite. Do not assert one exists.

- Uber India regional blog (older): *"riders will be charged a nominal wait-time fee when the pick-up exceeds 5
  minutes"* — https://www.uber.com/in/en/blog/wait-time-charges/ — **UNCERTAIN** (regional blog, undated in page body,
  conflicts with the current global 2-minute help page; illustrates only that thresholds vary by market).

### A2. Bolt — official waiting-time policy

- Exact wording: *"Paid wait time fee is a per-minute charge which starts a few minutes after a driver arrives at your
  location and continues until the driver starts the trip. This fee helps to compensate for the driver's efforts and
  time spent waiting. If your trip is cancelled and you are charged a cancellation fee, you won't be charged for the
  waiting time."* — **CONFIRMED (wording)**, https://bolt.eu/en/support/articles/360009458314/, retrieved 2026-10-07.
  **No free-minute number and no currency figure is published on this page.**
- Duplicate article with identical wording: https://bolt.eu/en/support/articles/4408148897810/ — **CONFIRMED**.
- A GB-market variant confirms the free allowance is **per ride category**, not universal: *"Once the free waiting
  period for your category has elapsed, a per-minute wait time fee will begin to accrue…"* —
  https://bolt.eu/en-gb/support/articles/360019510579/ — **CONFIRMED (that the threshold is category-specific)**,
  but **no number published**.

- **Disclosure mechanic (useful for C2):** Bolt states the charge appears *"In the app: Go to Activity from the app menu
  bar, select the intended ride, and find the Wait time line under Payment"* and *"On the receipt sent to your email
  (shown as Paid wait fee)"* — **CONFIRMED**, https://bolt.eu/en/support/articles/360009458314/.

## B. FARE ROUNDING CONVENTION IN UGX

### B1. Uber Uganda rounding — NOT FOUND (and there is counter-evidence)

- **NOT FOUND.** No source states that Uber Uganda rounded fares to a nearest denomination.
- All published Uber Uganda tariff figures at launch were exact multiples of UGX 100 (1,300 / 900 / 200 / 5,000), but a
  round tariff grid is **not** evidence of a rounding rule applied to computed trip totals.
- **Counter-evidence:** Uber Uganda's 2017 repricing produced Shs1,100 base and Shs750/km — neither is a multiple of
  500, and Shs750 is not even a multiple of 100. Uber Uganda's grid was therefore not locked to 100 or 500.
  Source for the repricing figures as above — **UNCERTAIN**.
- **Operational correction for anyone re-running this search:** the archived slug is
  `kampala-kampala-ug`, **not** `kampala-uganda`. Correct URL:
  https://web.archive.org/web/20240725123024/https://www.uber.com/global/en/r/cities/kampala-kampala-ug/
  These archived pages are marketing copy ("Getting Around Kampala: Ride, Earn, Eat | Uber") with **no fare breakdown and
  no rounding policy**. CDX shows snapshots only from **2024-07-25 onward** — there are no 2016–2023 captures of that page.

### B2. SafeBoda rounding — THE KEY FINDING

- **SafeBoda Uganda rounds prices to the nearest UGX 100.** — **CONFIRMED** (first-party; operator's own official channel)
- Exact quote: *"NOTE: SafeBoda prices are now being rounded off to the nearest 100shs, because of this, you may notice a
  slight change in your trip fare. Don't hassle with balance, your SafeBoda can send you your change as Cashless."*
- Sources: https://www.facebook.com/safeboda/posts/2683232001903388/ (SafeBoda Uganda official page)
  and the photo version of the same notice: https://www.facebook.com/safeboda/photos/2683231955236726/
- **Post date: NOT VERIFIED.** Facebook blocks unauthenticated fetch and the search proxy returned only the text
  snippet, not a timestamp. **Do not state a date for this.**
- Caveats: the post does not say whether it rounds up or down ("a slight change" implies either); and the operational
  rationale is explicit — eliminating the rider's need to produce/return small change, with cashless change offered instead.

### B3. General cash round-off convention

- **NOT FOUND as a documented rule.** No operator, regulator or industry body publishes a general "round off" convention
  for African ride-hail or mobile money.
- Behavioural corroboration only (**UNCERTAIN**, trade press, retrospective): *"Passengers learned how to negotiate.
  Drivers learned how to multi-home across platforms. Cash remained important."* —
  https://publicisteastafrica.com/uber-has-left-uganda-but-ride-hailing-has-already-won/, 2026-09-04. Supports the
  "change is real friction" rationale; is **not** a rounding rule.

### B4. Current practical minimum cash unit in Uganda

- **CONFIRMED (Wikipedia, current revision):** banknotes in circulation **1,000 / 2,000 / 5,000 / 10,000 / 20,000 /
  50,000**. Coins listed as *"Freq. used: 100, 200, 500, 1,000. Rarely used: 50"* —
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_shilling, retrieved 2026-10-07.
- The **UGX 50 coin** appears in the coin table as *"Issued 1998–2015"* while the infobox still lists it as
  "rarely used" — i.e. it sits at the edge of withdrawal. **The practical minimum cash unit in 2026 is UGX 100, not UGX 50.**
- **Two corrections to the task brief's premises:**
  1. "Smallest circulating unit is UGX 50" — **outdated as of 2026.**
  2. "500 Uganda shillings = UGX 1,000 was the old '1000' note" — this conflates a note with a coin. **UGX 500 is a
     coin** (nickel-plated steel, introduced 1998), not a note. The UGX 1,000 note is a separate denomination.
- Corroborating but non-official (**WEAK**): https://www.specialistuganda.com/uganda-information/uganda-currency
  (coins 50/100/200/500) and https://businesstimesug.com/what-you-need-to-know-about-the-uganda-currency/
  (notes 1,000–50,000).
- **Bank of Uganda could not be fetched** (https://www.bou.or.ug/currency_management returned a network failure). BoU is
  the authority here; this is a real gap.
- Ugandan news: Daily Monitor feature **"Uganda coins: Has the penny finally dropped?"**, Phillip Matogo, dated
  **Saturday, 10 January 2026** — lede: *"In 1998… the central bank introduced the Shs50 coin. Together with the Shs100,
  Shs200, and Shs500 coins, it was part of a new series of denominations…"* — https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/uganda-coins-has-the-penny-finally-dropped--5321958
  — **UNCERTAIN** (body paywalled; headline + lede confirm the 1998 coin series and ongoing coin-era uncertainty).
- A snippet attributed to Monitor — *"In June 2023, Bank of Uganda told the IMF that it was conducting a cost-benefit
  analysis to replace low-denomination notes with coins"* — **did not appear** in the fetched page body. **UNVERIFIED.**

## C. PEAK / NIGHT SURCHARGE

### C1. Which operators publish a peak mechanism, and at what hour

- **Bolt (official support):** *"When the demand for rides is high (e.g. peak hours, large-scale events, extreme weather
  conditions, etc.), rates are increased to encourage drivers to get on the road. When prices are increased, you will
  see an arrow pointing up next to the trip price. You can check the rates for each category by selecting it and clicking
  on the vehicle icon."* — **CONFIRMED**, https://bolt.eu/en/support/articles/115003390333/, retrieved 2026-10-07.
  **No clock hour is published.**
- **Bolt (official blog):** *"Peak hours usually occur before and after the 9-to-5 workdays, on weekend nights, during bad
  weather, and around major events like concerts or sports matches. Bolt prices tend to be lower outside of these busy
  times."* — **CONFIRMED**, https://bolt.eu/en/blog/bolt-pricing-explained/. Still no clock hour; "weekend nights" is the
  only night reference in any official Bolt text found.
- **Careem — Trip fare calculation (official):** *"Peak: Charges applied during Peak time, when there are more ride
  requests than available Captains. You will always be notified when it's a Peak time."* — **CONFIRMED**,
  https://help.careem.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040568534-Trip-fare-calculation (updated 2026-10-06).
- **Careem — Peak pricing (official), and the clearest disclosure mechanic found:** *"If peak pricing is being applied,
  this message will appear 'Fares are slightly higher than usual', after you confirm your booking details. Once you tap
  on this message, you will see the exact peak factor which will be applied to your total fare. The estimated fare for
  your selected car type will be shown with the peak pricing included."* — **CONFIRMED**,
  https://help.careem.com/hc/en-us/articles/6200322298771-Peak-pricing (edited 2026-09-29).
- **Uber: NOT FOUND** (official rider peak-pricing policy). `help.uber.com/en/riders/article/peak-pricing` and
  `/surge-pricing` both returned **404** as of 2026-10-07. Uber's help site now resolves articles via `?nodeId=` query
  params and the peak-pricing nodeId could not be resolved. Given Uber's wait-time page states rates are "applicable in
  that city", Uber clearly operates per-city peak pricing but does not publish a rider-facing global doc at a stable URL.
  **Do not fabricate an Uber peak-pricing figure.**
- **Africa-specific peak multiplier published anywhere: NOT FOUND.**

### C2. Ugandan night surcharge and its disclosure

- **SafeBoda Uganda — peak-hour fare increments, January 2019.** SafeBoda introduced them having previously *"always
  not had any trip increments during peak hours."* — **CONFIRMED (secondary, Ugandan tech press)**,
  https://techpointmag.com/safeboda-revises-trip-rates-peak-hours/ (Tech Point Magazine, last updated **24 Jan 2019**;
  archived copy https://web.archive.org/web/20210117084355/https://techpointmag.com/safeboda-revises-trip-rates-peak-hours/).
- **Disclosure mechanic — the directly useful answer:** *"Before requesting for a trip, the SafeBoda app will display the
  trip estimate as usual and increment any peak figures to the same trip cost… So, the new trip increments reflect on
  your app's trip fare."* → **the increment is folded into the PRE-REQUEST trip estimate shown before the rider
  confirms**, not surfaced as a post-hoc in-app badge and not disclosed only on the receipt.
- **No clock hour is published.** The same source says *"expect to see a rise in your usual evening trips mainly around
  the city center"* — evening framing, **no 19:00/20:00 start time given**.
- **A Ugandan night surcharge starting specifically at ~19:00 or 20:00: NOT FOUND.** No operator or news source
  reachable in this research publishes a fixed clock-hour night multiplier for Uganda.
- Context (not a surcharge): Bolt Uganda commission is **20% of final order price**; median Ugandan Bolt driver income
  **Shs359,083/week in Dec 2025** — https://publicisteastafrica.com/uber-has-left-uganda-but-ride-hailing-has-already-won/
  (2026-09-04) — **UNCERTAIN** (retrospective secondary).
- Uber exit (context, surfaced but not fetched): *"Uber is leaving Nigeria and Uganda, ending its ride-hailing services
  in the two African markets with immediate effect."* — https://www.africanews.com/2026/09/04/uber-exits-nigeria-and-uganda-as-competition-and-costs-rise/
  dated 2026-09-04 — **UNCERTAIN** (URL from search index; page not directly fetched).

## Industry-standard fallbacks (only where anchored to a real published number)

- **Free waiting time at pickup → use 2 minutes (5 for premium tiers).**
  UNSOURCED for East Africa specifically; industry fallback = **2 minutes**, anchored to Uber's official rider help
  page (https://help.uber.com/en/riders/article/wait-time-fees), which publishes 2 min for UberX and 5 min for Black.
- **UGX fare rounding → use nearest UGX 100.**
  Anchored to SafeBoda Uganda's own published rule: *"rounded off to the nearest 100shs"*
  (https://www.facebook.com/safeboda/posts/2683232001903388/). **Not 500.** Note Uber Uganda's own grid historically
  used non-multiples of 100 (Shs750), so UGX 100 is a defensible Ugandan operator convention but not a universal one.
- **Per-minute waiting rate in UGX → NO defensible current figure.**
  The only Uganda-specific per-minute figure ever published is **USh 200/minute (Uber Uganda, 2016 launch tariff)** —
  ten years stale, and Uber has since exited. I am deliberately offering **no** current UGX per-minute rate rather than
  extrapolating from stale data.
- **Peak/night surcharge → NO defensible clock hour.**
  No operator reachable here (Uber, Bolt, Careem, SafeBoda, Bolt Uganda) publishes a fixed night start hour. Careem's
  peak is demand-triggered ("more ride requests than available Captains") and explicitly notified to the rider;
  SafeBoda's is folded into the pre-request estimate. **Do not assert a 19:00 or 20:00 start as an industry norm.**
